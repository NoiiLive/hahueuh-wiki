Divine Protections

Thirty-nine blessings, handed out by the world when your character is born. Most are small and easy to miss. A few change how you play completely.

How you get one

Blessings are rolled once, the first time you join a world. You don't pick them and you can't train into them.

You get one roll at 25%. If it succeeds you get another at 15%, and if that succeeds one more at 5%. Then it stops. Four blessings isn't possible. Three in four players finish this process with nothing at all.

Only once a roll succeeds does the game pick how good the blessing is. Common is by far the most likely, Divine by far the least.

RarityHow many existChance when you win a roll
Common4about 62%
Rare16about 21%
Legendary12about 13%
Divine7about 4%
Nobody can roll a unique blessing off you. Once someone holds one of the five uniques it leaves the pool completely, so it can't be rolled by anyone else. It only changes hands through a command, through the Usurpation blessing, or if the holder dies on a hardcore world.

Common

Small conveniences. Nice to have, never decisive.

BlessingWhat it does
TelepathyLets you message any player by name, from anywhere, even across dimensions.
Lightning CloudLightning never hits you. The bolt is stopped outright, so you don't catch fire either.
Roasted FoodAnything you cook in a furnace comes out better than it should be.
Riding AnimalsShift and right click any animal to ride it, even ones you normally can't.

Legendary

These change how you fight or move all the time, not just occasionally.

BlessingWhat it does
CloudsYou can walk on open air, once you're at cloud height or above.
FireplayExplosions don't hurt you, and while you're on fire your stats surge.
Air DashingFalling doesn't hurt you. The landing turns into a dash instead.
Gathering SpiritsSpirits like you more, and rarer ones are more willing to answer.
Shedding BloodThe closer you are to death, the stronger you get.
Unarmed CombatYou fight like a master, as long as your hands are empty.
Weapon GodThe same, but only with a weapon in hand.
SunDuring the day every stat is raised and good potion effects hit harder.
MoonThe same, at night.
InitiativeThe first hit anyone lands on you does nothing, and your own first strike can't be dodged.
Second ComingOnce something has hit you, it can never hit you the same way again.
Anti-MalignanceMagical effects don't stick to you, and miasma does nothing.
Sun and Moon need a sky. In the Nether and the End there's no day or night, so neither blessing does anything at all.

Divine

These define a character. Five of the seven can only belong to one person at a time.

BlessingWhat it does
Phoenix You don't die. You come back where you fell, and usually keep the blessing with another "Next" added to its name.
First Sight You dodge everything, apart from attacks there's no dodging and attacks that land while you can't move.
Sword SaintUnique You're given the dragon sword Reid, which can't be broken. Only one is ever made per world.
SorceryUnique All six schools, a flawless Gate, and complete mastery of the Magic stat.
Death GodUnique You mark people. Marks turn into wounds that bleed, and nothing will close them.
UsurpationUnique Take a blessing off someone else and keep it.
Od-LagnaUnique All your magic is traded away. In return you earn blessings by living a certain way, and Phoenix arrives the instant you would first die, then renews forever.
Od-Lagna usually isn't available. It sits in the Divine pool but most servers remove it, because whoever holds it eventually collects nearly every blessing in the game.

Losing a blessing

Blessings are permanent under normal play. There are only four ways to lose one:

  • An admin takes it with a command.
  • Someone uses Usurpation on you.
  • You pick up a Witch Factor, which burns away every blessing you own at once.
  • You die on a hardcore world, which frees up any unique you were holding.

Phoenix is the odd one out. It gets used up when it saves you, and only stays if its roll comes up.
