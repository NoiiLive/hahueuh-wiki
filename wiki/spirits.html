Spirits overview

A spirit lends you its element and its magic. For the three players in four who were born with no magic at all, this is the only way in.

The four ranks

Rank decides everything about a spirit: how much mana it carries, how big a spell it can pay for, and how much it improves your casting while you have it.

RankManaBiggest spell it can fundBoost to your casting
Lesser250250Small
Quasi500500Moderate
Normal750750Large
Great15001500Huge

If you carry several spirits of the same element they add together rather than the best one winning, so a handful of Lesser spirits is worth having.

A Great spirit is worth as much as being max level. Its 1500 limit matches what a level 80 caster can do alone. This is how a brand new character gets to cast Al Karum or Ol Shamak.

Spirits grow too

Spirits gain levels up to 100, and a spirit that levels enough moves up a rank. Quasi at 15, Normal at 40, Great at 80. A Lesser spirit you look after is not permanently lesser.

Spirit Affinity

Affinity is a hidden number from 1 to 10 that your character is born with. It's never shown to you anywhere, and it's the single biggest factor in whether spirits will talk to you.

Your affinityChance a commune works
High75% to 90%
Middling30% to 40%
Low1%

That bottom row is not a typo. A character with low affinity will almost never get a spirit, and there's nothing they can do about it. The Gathering Spirits blessing raises your affinity and makes rarer spirits more likely to answer.

Spirit Commune

Commune costs 100 mana and has a ten minute cooldown. It doesn't need a school of magic, so nearly anyone can use it, and it can't fizzle no matter how weak your Gate is.

On success a spirit appears and offers itself. You can accept or turn it down, but either way you've spent the mana and started the cooldown.

Things that hurt your chances

  • Carrying miasma cuts your chance to roughly a third. Spirits don't like it. This is the main reason a heavy caster struggles to recruit.
  • Low mana halves your chance.
  • Already having lots of spirits costs you 20 percentage points for each one past your comfortable limit.

That comfortable limit depends on affinity, running from one spirit at the bottom to six at the top. Going past it doesn't block anything, it just makes each new spirit harder to get. Someone with high affinity can realistically gather around eleven. Someone on the 1% rate will struggle to get their first.

What actually turns up

Nine times in ten the spirit shares an element you already use, because spirits are drawn to something familiar. Rank is mostly luck, and mostly disappointing:

Casting with a spirit

When you cast, the game pays for the spell in order: your elemental pools first, then your spirit's own reserves, then your own mana. Each one casts at its own speed and efficiency, so one spell can be part funded by a spirit and part by you.

A spirit doesn't get around your Gate. Magic a spirit paid for still has to leave through you. A No Release Gate stops it exactly as it stops your own.
Spirits have to be nearby. A spirit only helps within 16 blocks, and one sitting in an unloaded chunk can't help at all even if the map says it's close. If yours has gone quiet, try /rezero spirit <player> recall.

Temporary contracts

Temporary Contract rolls up to five short lived spirits at once. Each roll is 5 points more likely to succeed than a permanent commune, and crucially they ignore the crowding penalty completely.

That makes them the reliable option once you already have a few spirits, and the usual way players reach a spell that's normally out of range. See Anchors and contracts.
