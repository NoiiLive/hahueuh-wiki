Magic overview

You have two pools of magical energy. One refills and one doesn't, and confusing them will end your character's casting career permanently.

The six schools

Fire, Water, Earth, Wind, Yin and Yang. There are three ways to get one: be born with it, receive it from a blessing, or borrow it from a spirit.

Only Yin has spells written for it. The other five schools work in every way described above, but there's nothing to cast in them yet. See the spell list.

Mana and Od

Mana is what you spend. It refills, quickly, by standing still and gathering it from the air around you.

Od is what you're made of. It only refills when you die. Casting never touches it on purpose, but if a spell needs more mana than you have left, the difference comes out of your Od instead.

Running your Od down to zero cripples you for good. You end up withered, and your Insanity jumps straight to maximum. There's no cure, and it's meant that way. A server that finds this too harsh can soften it into a twenty minute magic lockout instead.

The practical advice is simple. Watch your mana bar during long casts. If it's going to run out, stop.

Why your race decides how much you can hold

Both pools come from how long your character is going to live. Since everyone starts Human, everyone starts small.

Training Magic multiplies your mana by up to two and a half times, and you can safely carry more than your limit for short periods. Even so, a Human is badly stretched by the biggest spells. Ol Shamak costs 1250, which most Humans simply cannot hold.

This is what spirits are for. A spirit brings its own mana and its own size limit. It's how a short lived character ever casts anything expensive. See Spirits.

Gathering mana

Holding the gather action pulls mana out of the chunk you're standing in, refilling about a tenth of your total every second.

Chunks aren't bottomless. Each holds a limited amount and takes around three in game days to recover, so a spot you keep casting in really does run dry. Move somewhere fresh.

You can deliberately overfill yourself, which is how people get enough mana for a spell they're too small for. The first 50% over your limit is free. Past that you start building strain, and enough strain destroys your Gate. The absolute ceiling is double your normal maximum.

A No Absorption Gate can't gather at all, and neither can a Destroyed one.

Casting, and the three ways it can fail

  1. Your Gate is in the wrong state. Destroyed blocks everything. No Release blocks all six schools.
  2. The spell is too big for your level. Your Magic level caps how large a spell you can attempt alone.
  3. The spell is too fast for your Output. If it wants to draw more than three times what you can push, it fizzles and the mana is wasted.

All three are explained properly on The Gate.

Where the mana comes from

The game pays for a spell in order rather than mixing everything together. It uses your elemental spirit pools first, then a contracted spirit's own reserves, then your mana, and finally your Od.

Each source casts at its own speed and its own efficiency, so a single spell can genuinely be half paid for by a spirit and half by you.
